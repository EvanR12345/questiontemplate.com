# Narration, motion and director review — October 3, 2026

## Implemented repairs

The existing Kokoro model and helper environment are preserved. Delivery version 3 routes unsupported whole vocal cries (Ahhh, Aaagghhh, Ugh) to brief pauses. This keeps the author's source text and the director's story evidence intact; it does not pretend to synthesize acted screams. Meaningful dialogue such as No, Help, contractions and names remains spoken. Marked effects and isolated cues such as Thud/Slash become effects, while ordinary prose containing those words remains narration. Effects previously peaked at 0.5 regardless of the voice. The new cap is 0.12, or 0.08 for whooshes, and is reduced further for quiet surrounding narration. Users can select Subtle or Off.

Settings now contains bounded local voice auditions, including Fenrir, Puck and the current Michael voice. Auditions are persisted and cached separately; they do not overwrite chapter WAVs or timelines. Distinct voices may queue together; repeated clicks for the same audition are deduplicated. Stock voices are comparisons, not verified matches or clones of a channel narrator.

Moving shots now use fractional source-corner sampling with cubic interpolation instead of moving integer zoompan crops. A 240-frame fixed-marker test reduced RMS frame acceleration from 0.19269 to 0.00380 pixels/frame² (98%). This is a motion-jitter measurement, not a universal perceptual-quality score. The output stays full-frame, and intentional pans/zooms remain available. Changed moving clips have separate cache identities; old shaky motion is not silently reused. Static clips retain their cache behavior. Previous videos and assets remain saved.

## Luna as director

Actual saved plans used GPT-6 Luna for casting, story analysis, chapter pacing, scene direction, camera decisions, workflow selection, continuity review, prompts and image QC. Kokoro generated speech; FLUX.2 Klein 4B generated the pictures. Luna did not author the original special-request intro script; that was authored separately, as requested, and Luna visualized it.

Luna produced 103 story shots with meaningful scene purposes and varied intentional camera movement. Its QC results were 42 passed, 21 requiring review and 40 failed. These are the model's assessments, not independent proof that 61 images are unusable. Its useful findings include a hammer where a bat was required, a visible blade where a gun was expected, missing restraints and shooting in the wrong direction. Some demands—exactly ten distinguishable fallen people, or every carried injury/prop in a frame—are difficult or potentially over-strict. One gun omission was traced to the application's visibility filter, not exclusively to Luna or FLUX, and was fixed separately. A plan's expectations, the actual recorded generation prompt and the resulting image must be compared before assigning blame.

The result is usable creative planning with significant visual inconsistencies requiring review. Switching director models alone will not remove image-conditioning limits, rendering defects or unsupported vocalizations. A future controlled comparison should ask Luna and Sol to direct the same bounded difficult scene with identical canonical state, references and image budget, then compare story errors, prompt clarity, repair count, latency and total cost. No paid Sol comparison was run.

[GPT-6 Luna](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-6-luna) lists $0.10/M input and $0.50/M output tokens; [GPT-6 Sol](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-6-sol) lists $2/M input and $10/M output. Sol is 20× the per-token price at those standard rates. A selective chapter-plan or complex-action review is a more economical trial than replacing every routine check with Sol. Equal token counts or fewer repairs cannot be assumed in advance.

## Why this run was slow and expensive

Saved final image-model calls total 3:55, averaging 2.28 seconds each. Completed image queue jobs total 10:28 including reference upload, setup, transfers and saving. Completed QC jobs total 24:13, including repeated checks; final saved QC calls alone total 16:50. Repeated failed preparation/director attempts added more wall time. Fresh GPU rental was 32:52 including startup/setup and idle intervals, at approximately $2.111/hour. Latest pre-repair rendering/assembly totaled approximately 5:42 locally. These measurements overlap: rental includes generation; parent stages include their component passes. They must not all be added together.

The earlier cumulative estimate of roughly $1.77 includes rejected work and retained storage, so multiplying it by video duration is not a stable per-video production price. This repair uses no paid image generation, vision checks, director requests or GPU rental. Storage remains billed independently while stopped.

Preserve the existing 5.85 images/minute: a two-hour video needs about 702 images. Scaling measured image queue work gives roughly 71 minutes of active image-job time. At the old rental rate that is about $2.51 of compute before startup/repairs, rather than $10. At a hypothetical same-throughput $0.99/hour worker it is about $1.18. Full Luna API use scaled from this run is around $0.6/day, and 71 GB storage is about $0.17/day at the recorded hourly rate. A $2–4 two-hour-video target is plausible only if setup/idle time and bounded repairs stay small; it is not a benchmarked guarantee.

[Runpod published pricing](https://www.runpod.io/pricing) lists 5090 at $0.99/hour and 4090 at $0.74/hour. The read-only live catalog also reported cheaper community prices, but availability was low. Crucially, no 4090/5090 stock was reported in the existing US-NE-1 volume region. Cheaper compute needs a deliberate model-cache/region migration plan, whose transfer, startup and storage costs must be included. No resource was created or started for this investigation.

Best next optimizations: prepare narration/director work before rental; reuse loaded model/reference encodings; generate continuously, then stop compute before QC/rendering; reuse cached checks for unchanged inputs; benchmark bounded batches against sequential throughput rather than loading three duplicate pipelines blindly. Keep full checks for high-quality mode. Sampled checks can reduce latency in an explicit faster mode, but unchecked frames must remain labeled unchecked. Keep the image model and image cadence; multi-panel canvases are unnecessary to obtain savings from avoidable overhead.

Validation: 60 focused Python tests passed, plus the real 240-frame FFmpeg jitter comparison and a saved-shot 720p preview. Local stock auditions were synthesized with the existing helper. No Torch/CUDA/environment replacement or image-model download was performed.
